You straighten, steadying your breathing and scrubbing your hand through your hair. The breeze chills the sweat against your skin, unpleasantly clammy, but welcome and *grounding* after that whole... experience.

A deep breath, and exhale. A cleanse of your Soul Gem, a weight lifted off your shoulders.

"So..." Sayaka's voice jolts you from your thoughts, your friend peering at you with a skeptical but concerned look. "What was that? Some kind of mental attack?"

"Nah," you say, shaking your head. "Not an attack from the Wraith." You wave your hand in the direction of where the Wraith had been, just to emphasize what you mean. "That's, uh, what I call them, by the way. That was me having a... negative Eureka moment, you know? An Akerue moment - an epiphany but in a negative way."

"Right, right," Sayaka says, stepping closer and squinting at you, her hands on her hips. "You're *sure* you're OK? You look kind of shaken, whatever that whole thing was."

"I'm alright *now*," you say, taking another deep breath. "It's difficult to put in words, but just... the feeling of someone walking on my grave, you know? But as I said, it wasn't the Wraith itself, just that *seeing* it made me realize some things about my magic." You grimace. "And it wasn't even anything *bad*, just unsettling. I'll tell you about it in a moment, just... let me gather myself."

"Oh, like taking bio class," Sayaka says, nodding. "Realizing all those weird squishy things are happening inside your body."

"... yeah, actually," you say. "Kind of like that, except in my soul."

"*Sometimes* I know what I'm talking about," Sayaka says, grinning and buffing her fingernails on her costume.

"Give yourself more credit," you say. "*Most* of the time. Also, uh, let me just-"

Sayaka rolls her eyes.

"That's it, see if I ever do anything nice for you again," she says.

Even as she says that, she still trails half a step behind you as you walk over to the Grief Cube, fallen from the remains of the Wraith. You'd been too distracted to really think about it, earlier, but you'd felt the way reality closed around the Wraith as it disintegrated, the way the world had closed around the spot like a scab healing over a wound.

You take a moment to think, mentally replaying the formation of the miasma and the dispersal of the wraith, and the formation of the Grief Cube. And dispersal *is* the right word, you think. A wraith doesn't need a soul to form, that much you know and that much you'd just observed, which means that there's nothing more to hold onto the Grief. Grief is... a *weight* upon the world, and in that analogy, the miasma and wraith are the singularity forming around sufficient mass. Or perhaps nuclear fission would be more accurate: the initiation of criticality by unthinking processes when sufficient mass is gathered.

And in that analogy... the Grief Cube is what happens when the wraith is destroyed and the Grief, the nuclear material is scattered and submerged beneath the skin of the world to drain away, but there the analogy breaks down. A Grief Cube is, in some ways, the *inverse* of nature abhoring a vacuum, because a Grief Cube is a scar in reality that had *too much* Grief, and now has too *little*. A point of stable, *less-than-ambient* pressure.

"What's that?" Sayaka asks, leaning over to look at the little midnight-black cube, cradled in your hand.

"That, my dear Sayaka," you say as a smile spreads across your face, your glee barely contained. "Is the solution to all our problems."

"Doesn't look much like a trig answer key," Sayaka says immediately.

"... the solution to *many* of our problems," you say, as if you hadn't ever said otherwise. That would just be silly hyperbole, and you *never* engage in dramatic hyperbole, not even in your own thoughts. "It's a Grief Cube. Like a Grief Seed, but much lesser capacity."

"So you can cleanse with that?" Sayaka asks, her eyes narrowing at you. "And... oh. *Oh*. And it-" Her voice drops to a whisper, excitement starting to spread across her face as understanding dawns. "It doesn't come from a Witch."

"*Exactly*," you say. The excitement is infectious, and your grin splits your face, the butterflies in your stomach threatening to escape. "Sayaka, this could be *everything* we needed. I-" You bite your lip. "I have to caution that we need to *test* this, make sure it works how I expect it to, see how we can farm them effectively, but..."

"Yeah. *Yeah*," Sayaka says, her eyes bright. "Yeah, *OK*, this was worth the science time."

You snort.

"What, as if figuring out that you *can* copy my magic and *cleanse yourself* wasn't worth it?" you say, raising an eyebrow at her. "'cuz I think that's damn important too."

"Bah, that could have happened at any time," Sayaka says, waving her hand dismissively - but the way her grin manages to widen a little more gives lie to her nonchalance. "But that's big too. Just not as big. But *man* I can't wait to get out of class so I can test this out."

"Damn right it is, and damn right you'll figure it out," you say. "I'll give you what advice I can there. And we're gonna go back and tell the others how awesome we both are, *but* there's a couple things I want to confirm here before we make a move. Some of it I can test on the go, but some of it I'd rather test while the chance of collateral is minimal."

"Like what happens when you fill that Grief Cube up on Grief?" Sayaka says, shuddering. "I mean, we know what happens with Grief *Seeds*..."

"Yeah," you say. "It... *should* form a new miasma and a new wraith. Maybe *more* wraiths? I'm... there *is* one discrepancy from what I expected, which is that I kind of expected more Grief Cubes out of this? But then again, it might just be that I gave it the absolute bare minimum of Grief that it needed to form, so maybe that has something to do with it."

"Sure, sure, I get what you mean," Sayaka says with a shrug. "Gotta make sure that it *works* as expected."

"So! First things first, I'm gonna, uh," you say, looking down at your conspicuously clean Soul Gem, freshly cleansed. Then you look at Sayaka, who's a clone. "Uh. I'm gonna practice a few generic magic blasts or something until I've got enough Grief to test this."

Sayaka snickers.

"Sure, sure," she says.

"Yeah..." you say sheepishly. You *could* enchant some Grief, or practice some Grief tricks or something, but the problem with anything along those lines is that they're *efficient* - and you want to be *wasteful* right now.

And so, you focus for a moment, gathering magic to your palm, and with a twist of your mind, launch it forth as a spear of silvery light which hisses across the courtyard to impact on some sandy tiles. You... manage to kick some sand into the air.

"You're even worse with that than Homura is," Sayaka observes, plopping herself back down on her stone seat to watch.

"I mean, yeah," you say, flicking another bolt with a backhand swing to impact on the same spot. "I don't *actually* want to do any damage. Just a light show. And it's not exactly my speciality, throwing around magic like this."

"Not hers, either," Sayaka says, thoughtful. "I suppose she didn't *really* want to actually hurt... the Incubator, back then."

"I mean, I don't exactly know what she was thinking at the time, but probably something along the lines of trying to keep the noise down?" you say. "Guns are loud an' all."

"Yeah, I suppose," Sayaka concedes. "Suppose she knew we were nearby, and didn't want to spook us too badly."

"Probably, yeah," you agree. And speaking of guns, you raise both hands, index fingers pointing, and blast away sequentially, silvery bolts of magic sizzling across the courtyard and doing little more than raising more puffs of dust.

It's ruinously wasteful on magic - you estimate less than a quarter of the magic you put into each bolt actually making it to the target. And not to mention the lack of any actually useful payload to deal damage. You *could* definitely improve it more, or at least launch bigger bolts, but you're really just trying to waste magic here.

"Also, mm..." you say in between bursts of magic. "The thing I realized is that... well, I can control Grief via controlling my own Witch. Or like... sharing the Grief control aspect of my Witch."

"... ah," Sayaka says. "*Ah*. Yeah, OK, yeah. That's why you looked like you'd seen a ghost."

"Sort of did, yeah," you say, sighing.

"Wait, does that mean-" Sayaka frowns. "... so I managed to copy your magic *because* I know about Witches now, and... what, subconsciously accepted that?"

"There's still some... weird stuff about my magic *I* don't understand," you say, grimacing. "But... yeah. Something like that? More of an understanding of Grief and Witches and such."

"Hrm," Sayaka says, frowning and falling silent.

The experience of having Dedolere looming in the depths of your Soul is something you can only compare to what it might be to look down at your own grave. You can't help but glance at your Soul Gem with your mind's eye, feeling that sepulchral whisper ghosting down your spine, and thankfully, there's nothing different. Dedolere slumbers still at the root of your Soul, but then again...

That is not dead which can eternal lie.

Your drama dial might be tuned a little high at the moment, but it feels terribly applicable.

And yet despite the realization of your powers, your magic doesn't feel any different. Grief moves to your will, flowing into a sphere, then a cube, then a cylinder, then dispersing back into raw Grief and rotating back into your hammerspace as naturally as crooking a finger, as naturally as breathing. The magic blasts continue to sizzle from your hands, and rain down upon the courtyard, exactly as wastefully inefficient as before.

It doesn't take you long before you can feel the sheen of Grief upon your Soul, a thin, cloying film tugging at your mind. Perfect for your first test.

"Alright," you announce, drawing Sayaka's attention once more. "I've got a good amount of Grief in my Soul Gem, so, time for testing."

"Cool," Sayaka says, straightening on the stone seat.

You can feel her attention on you as you bring the Grief Cube back to your hand: knowing what you know, it's the *result* of having Grief form a miasma and a wraith, but it's not *directly* Grief. Not something you can replicate directly, and not something that actually responds to your Grief control. But it was simple enough to wrap it in a thin sheen of Grief to hold it and free up your hands as you'd burned magic, returning now to your hand at your call.

You reach for your Soul Gem, holding it in the other, take a breath, and bring them together.

And you watch as silently, without fuss, the Grief drains from your Soul and into the Cube in sullen little streams. It feels not much different from using a Grief Seed, nor any different from your never-was memory of Homura using a handful of Grief Cubes. After a moment, the absorption stops, cutting off entirely at what must be the Cube's limit: just about a fiftieth of your Soul Gem's capacity, or about four marbles.

You let out a breath you hadn't realized you were holding in.

"That's that, then?" Sayaka asks. "It works?"

"... yeah," you say. A thought wipes away the remaining Grief in your Soul Gem as you start to grin again. "*Yeah*, it works. It *works*!"

You find yourself bouncing on your feet, practically dancing with energy.

There are still so many things to figure out. How to make more, whether more can be made without you, whether they can be made *safely*, but this *is* the start. You still need to prepare for Walpurgisnacht. You still need to make connections, make allies and *friends* across Japan, across the world, who share your ideals. But here, now, this one ordinary day has suddenly become *momentous*.

You're getting ahead of yourself.

"OK," you say, checking the time again. "Running a bit short on time, but there's one last thing I absolutely have to understand before we can head off: what *happens* with filled Grief Cubes? I mean, a filled Grief Seed re-hatches the Witch, so... I sort of expect this to spawn a new miasma. Maybe even a bigger one."

"Which would mean we could potentially farm these things?" Sayaka asks.

"Hopefully, yeah," you say.

And to that end, you find your eyes drawn to the Cube, now sitting on the palm of your hand and all but glowing with malevolent energy. You bite your lip, staring at it.

It's not forming a new miasma. Maybe that's something that takes a while to happen? Or maybe...

"There's a suction to it, even though it's full," you murmur thoughtfully. Not the same kind of negative pressure of an empty Grief Cube, but something different. Something you're familiar with. "So..."

"It'll soak up Grief in the air?" Sayaka asks.

"I think so," you say, flicking the Cube upwards. Your Grief control seizes upon its contents to carry it straight up, a sphere of Grief drawn from hammerspace following it. "I think it'll soak up ambient Grief and form a miasma? Roughly consistent with Grief Seeds, too - a regular Grief Seed draws Grief from a Clear Seed, because... Grief calls to Grief, and it flows from lower to higher concentrations."

"Makes a *sort* of sense," Sayaka says, making a face. "Metaphysics."

"Metathaumics?" you offer. You frown thoughtfully, watching the Cube and the marble soar up into the sky. "Also, you know what? We're missing a third form of Grief. Maybe *at least* a third form. I've got Grief sphere, we've just discovered Grief Cubes, it stands to reason there's got to be an in-between Grief tetrahedron of some kind."

"Uh-huh," Sayaka says, giving you a dubious look, as if she can't figure out if you're serious or not. "And a Grief octahedron, dodecahedron, icosahedron and so on, right?"

"Exactly," you say, nodding seriously. "That's research for another day, though, because for now-"

You direct her gaze upwards to the Grief Cube and marble, both hovering at the furthest limit of your range once more. You notice that the ambient Grief that had been in the area seems to have largely dispersed in the wake of the wraith being destroyed, and even the more 'settled' Grief seems to have mostly drained away. Not all of it, but a lot of it - it seems that the settling process makes it harder to disperse. Which largely makes sense, with the way you're thinking of it as something akin to water soaking into the ground, and more than fine for your purposes, but certainly something to keep in mind.

With Sayaka's attention on you, you shove both items past your control limit: the Cube with a mental flick of your wrist, launching it straight up, and the marble of Grief with a convulsive jerk to let it go. And you watch in your mind's eye as the marble explodes into a cloud, billowing and dark... and all but immediately, starts gathering around the Grief Cube. You frown - it doesn't *absorb* any more, but the Grief gathers around it and *stays* in a way you haven't seen before.

You frown.

It's gathering *more* Grief than one marble's worth, you notice, and it takes you a moment to realize where from - it's drawing in the more settled Grief that you'd noticed, the 'groundwater' Grief, draining and gathering it to itself. Which is useful to know, since it means you've got a way to clean that stuff up beyond grabbing it yourself.

The Cube arcs downwards and back into your control radius, and you grab both the Cube and its gathered cloud of Grief before they can fall too much further.

"An empty Cube draws Grief *into* itself because it has... negative pressure of sorts," you murmur, half to yourself and half to Sayaka. "But a *full* Cube gathers Grief to itself because Grief calls to Grief - but without sucking more into the Cube *itself*. So a full Cube won't 'hatch' into a wraith, but it *will* suck in Grief to *form* a new wraith."

Which is moderately dangerous, because people who aren't yourself -or Sayaka- can't actually *tell* when there's Grief gathering around a Cube, or how much. An annoying problem, but something that can be accounted for. Still, you need to confirm your hypothesis, which means more Grief.

You rotate another hundred marbles from hammerspace, and launch them skywards.

It doesn't take anything more than that, and you watch as the Grief draws in towards the Cube, even the 'groundwater' Grief flooding towards it like water flowing downhill. It doesn't take more than a few moments before the Cube... dissolves. You feel reality shiver and buckle once more, and-

*Danger.*

You're already sidestepping to your left, Sayaka bounding right as shimmering void slashes down from the skies. Above you is a wraith, a barely-human form with just a torso and a vague blob in the suggestion of shapes, an empty spear wielded in one hand. You get the impression of its split face peering at you past the glittering rainbow pixellation, the empty, roaring hunger of its existence pressing against your mind.

As it prepares to launch its spear again, you catch Sayaka's eye, wave her down, and command the wraith to die.

Without fuss, without protest, it unravels. You're not sure that saying that it *died* is accurate, to be perfectly honest, because wraiths aren't really alive - but that's a matter to consider later. You keep your attention on the process, memorizing it to think about later, and you watch as the miasma disperses instantly, draining outwards faster than you've ever seen it.

"Nasty things," Sayaka says in the silence that reigns in the wake of the wraith.

"Yeah..." you say, frowning thoughtfully as you look around. "Not as bad as Witches, though.... did you see a Cube drop anywhere?"

"Nope," Sayaka says. "Uh."

You frown, scanning the entire area with your Grief-sense. You *know* you can sense the things, and you're not seeing it.

"Maybe it was the way I killed it?" you hazard. "Gonna try a new one, just to be sure."

"Right," Sayaka says, likewise frowning.

You're already launching up fresh Grief drawn from your hammerspace. At the very least these experiments are depleting the amount of Grief you're holding onto - you're a little worried about *pollution*, but that's something you'll have to investigate later. For now, the quantities you're working with are small enough that you're not too concerned, and you want to understand wraiths and Grief Cubes.

A hundred more marbles, cast into the sky, and the response is almost immediate: the formation of another wraith in all its ghastly pallour, its attendant miasma tiny and barely there, and it's one more akin to the *first* wraith you'd created, sitting passively upon nothing. Once more, you reach out with your mind and command it to end, and it does so.

*This* time, as the wraith shatters and the Grief disperses, you spot the crystalline shimmer of a Grief Cube falling from the sky.

"... so, *not* the way you killed it," Sayaka says, frowning as her eyes track the falling Cube.

"Seems not," you say, likewise frowning. "I think... well, I've been mentally describing to these things are kind of 'scars' on reality, with the 'wound' being too much Grief in one place. Like a kind of rebound effect, I guess. Or like... the wraith makes a small crater, but if you pile on more Grief and make a bigger, badder wraith, the crater walls collapse entirely."

"And it can't, uh, scar over twice?" Sayaka hazards.

"Seems like it," you say, scowling as you reach out to snag the falling Cube. "So it means... no second order Grief Cubes. No *farming* them; we're gonna have to figure out how to get, uh, free-range wraiths."

Sayaka nods.

"That doesn't sound too bad," she says. "Honestly... it sounds like... mm. Witches suck up Grief as well, right? So it sounds like if we want wraiths, then it's necessary to keep an area *free* of Witches for a good amount of time."

"Which doesn't sound too awful," you agree, nodding. "Though I have to say... well, all this is one afternoon's work. Damn promising, but something that needs a bit more examination, to see whether it scales correctly and all. And keeping an entire city clear of Witches... well, it's a good thing to do regardless. Difficult, but good."

"... yeah," Sayaka says, her expression darkening.

You exhale, bouncing the Grief Cube in your free hand as you check the time on your phone again.

"At least it gives us a confirmed way to dispose of Grief Cubes, I guess," you say. "That said, I think that's everything I need to know right now, *and* that's all the time we have now. There are other experiments to do, but nothing I can't do on the move. So... back to school?"

"Well, *you* need to head to school," Sayaka says. She grins, and lobs her keyring of power jewels at you. "See you there."

You catch the ring, rolling your eyes.

"Yeah, yeah, you big lazy bum," you say. "See you there. And just so you know, you were a great help today. And I mean that sincerely. Just being here and keeping watch for me and letting me have that mental security was an enormous help."

"Ah, happy to help, don't worry about it," Sayaka grumbles, looking away awkwardly. "You were doing me a favour too, to give me something to keep me awake in class, you know?"

"Still!" you say. "Thanks, Sayaka. I'm always glad you've got my back."

Your only answer is a lazy flap of her hand as her clone unravels into wisps of cerulean blue.

You exhale, bouncing on your toes, and take one last look around Warehouse-kun to make sure nothing's wrong - no lingering wraiths, or stray magical constructs, or anything of the sort. The level of ambient Grief is perhaps rather higher than elsewhere, but killing the wraiths spread the Grief pretty effectively, and you can tell it's draining away steadily as opposed to trying to clump back up. That seems mostly in order, then: long-term Grief pollution is definitely something to be concerned about, you think, but this can be your first case study. You take a moment to memorize the distribution, the levels of Grief here, and the next time you return, you'll be able to do a comparison.

Alright.

With nothing left here that needs attending to, you let your wings bloom and take flight, the Grief Cube tucked safely in your pockets.

And *that* thought, the almost imperceptible weight in your pocket, pastes a grin firmly on your face as you soar past Mitakihara's skyline. Grief Cubes. *Grief Cubes*.

Distributable cleansing that doesn't require Witches, doesn't require people *Witching out*. Distributable cleansing that comes with built-in disposal, even - sure, it would've been nice if you could just spawn more wraiths using a filled Grief Cube, and recycle them infinitely, but at the same time, it means that you'd have an ever-scaling number of Cubes. Having a consistent way of disposing them is pretty good, too.

Speaking of disposing of Grief Cubes, you're reminded of how that memory of a future never to come had ended, with Homura tossing the filled Cubes to Kyuubey for disposal. If the Incubator is aware of your experiments, you certainly haven't seen it, and either it somehow hasn't noticed, or it doesn't care.

It doesn't seem likely that it wouldn't care about your developments here. A Witchless alternative to Grief Seeds *will* change the world, to the detriment of the Incubators. You know that it is, after all, less efficient for them.

Nor does it seem likely that your experiments have gone without notice. You've made no particular secret of your intention to rip its payday away from it, after all, so you can't imagine that it's not keeping tabs on you. You're not going to let Madoka contract, and nor are you going to stop fighting for a world where no magical girl will ever become a Witch. And you didn't cover this experiment in a shroud of secrecy because you *can't*, almost by definition: it's an experiment that *required* you to work beyond your own range.

And yet you've seen neither hide nor hair of the alien.

You're not entirely sure why, and that leaves you with a deep sense of unease.

And speaking of unease, your mind can't help but wander back to the *realization* you came to today. You control Grief. You control Grief by *borrowing* the Grief control of your own Witch, or perhaps sharing. Maybe you're *controlling* your own Witch, puppeting her slumbering form, but you're not sure that's entirely right.

You're more aware of her than most magical girls are, you think. Maybe even *uniquely* aware of her, of the way she sleeps at the root of your own soul, a speck of abyssal nightmare. *Asleep*, and you... you *know* when she stirs. But therein lies the crux, because at the root of your own soul is still part of your soul. She's a part of you, or maybe *you're* a part of *Dedolere*, aaaand that's something you're going to stop thinking about now.

You roll your shoulders and rub your arms, trying to tell yourself that wave of goosebumps and that chill you feel is just the wind rushing past you in flight, the cold wind finding its way up your sleeves and beneath your coat. Or maybe it's just your imagination. Entirely psychosomatic, that's the trick. *Entirely* psychosomatic. Getting back on point, then...

Given that you'd Wished to control *all* Grief, that means your Witch can too. But there's a unique exception to your Witch being asleep, isn't there? You'd created a Barrier. *Your* Barrier, yours to do with, to mold as you want to. But also *her* Barrier. And then you'd had a... sharp emotional shock, with Mami's reaction. And then there was The Feather.

Your Witch had a moment of influence, of *control*, the tiniest sliver of weight upon reality... and, perhaps, reality responded. Responded in the same was as the formation of Grief Cubes.

It's a lot of conjecture, true. Unprovable conjecture, and one with a hole in the shape of 'why a totally normal feather' at that, but it doesn't feel wrong - missing a step or two, at worst. It's something you'll have to keep in mind, as you begin to finally understand more of your magic: that there are dangers which your power is uniquely poised to uncover.

But then again, you can't say it was a waste.

*Grief Cubes*.

*And* Sayaka managing to do a copy of your magic - a partial copy, but that's still a hell of a thing!

You carry that bubbling sense of glee all the way to Mitakihara Middle School, practically vibrating as you wait for Mami and the rest of your friends, and by the time the schoolbell rings, you're all but bouncing on the spot, eyes eagerly fixed on the school doors and waiting for your friends to emerge. Students begin to spill out of the doors, and *there's* the bright honey-gold of Mami's hair, darting through the crowd.

You greet her with a beaming grin and open arms, Mami crashes into you with a laugh, trusting you to catch her. And catch her you do, lifting her off the ground in a tight hug, ignoring the murmurs and amused looks sent your way - it seems you've gained a little notoriety amongst the other students, given the way you're there every day, before and after classes.

"Hey," you murmur into Mami's hair.

"Hey yourself," Mami murmurs right back. She pulls back, peering up at you with a smile and curious eyes. "You seem to be in a good mood."

"Well, it's a day where I get to hug Tomoe Mami," you say, grinning as you set her back down on her feet. "But also, we managed to do some Exciting Science."

"You'll have to tell me about it," Mami says.

"Absolutely," you say, snickering as a stray thought strikes you. "Also, have you ever thought about participating in multiversal competitions? You'd do amazing."

"... what kind of competitions?" Mami asks.

"I'm not sure, but I bet we'd rock the cooking part," you say, nodding firmly.

"Ah, *that* sort of competition," Mami says thoughtfully. "I *have* thought it would be nice to participate in a gameshow sometime?"

"Then it's settled," you say. "And- oh, hey, everyone!"

"Hello Mami, Sabrina!" Madoka chirps back, joining you alongside Hitomi and Sayaka, Homura trailing a step behind with a faintly puzzled look on her face.

You flash Homura a quick grin and a thumbsup. She stares at you for a long moment, then shrugs, exhaling slowly in an imperceptible sigh - she'll accept your reassurance that this latest bullshit is a good thing, at least until you can explain it in full to her.

"Good afternoon, everyone," Mami says, taking a half step so she can face her friends without splitting away from your side.

"Good afternoon, Mami, Sabrina," Hitomi says, waving as they approach through the crowds of students drifting away from the school.

"Hello, Madoka, Hitomi, Homura, and Sayaka, who I definitely haven't seen elsewhere being out of school during school hours," you say gravely.

Sayaka rolls her eyes.

"I am a *model student* who never skips class," she says. "Right, Madoka? Back me up here."

"Yup, yup, though Mami and I won't be staying for long," you say. You toss Sayaka her power jewels as you all start walking. "Got that appointment in Kagoshima we've got to make."

"Ahh, I want to join you for one of these excursions one day," Madoka says wistfully. "Travelling like that must be nice!"

"... honestly... pencil that in for a conversation another day?" you say, catching Homura's eye and nodding slightly. You'll respect her wishes on this one, but it's something to be discussed. Her eyes sharpen, but there's no real protest on her expression - a discussion, not a decision. "There's practical problems we'll have to sort out for that, but it's not like they're all dangerous, so I don't think it's out of the question."

"We'll be glad to have you," you say, beaming. "Anyway! Real quick, since we've got to split off shortly if we're gonna make our visits today - Sayaka, are you coming?"

"Uhhhh," Sayaka says intelligently. "Uhhhhhhhhhhhhhhh."

"Nope!" Madoka says. "Sayaka's been skiving off enough today, I'm calling dibs."

"Gotta respect dibs," you say gravely. "But... well. We *are* visiting other groups today, and even if we can't come to an agreement over an alliance, they might let Sayaka expand her collection of powers."
