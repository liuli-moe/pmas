# Riding Shotgun pt. 7

Spoiler: Vote in abeyance

- [x] Thank Sayaka for the advice. It's good advice and you're kinda really very new to this whole thing.
- [x] Continue the Grief release experiment

"What's next is that I thank you for the advice," you say with a grin. "I don't think I actually *said* it, so... thank you."

Sure, this latest discovery is huge. It *is*, and you can feel the excitement bubbling up from within you as it threatens to crack your expression. But at the same time, it's Sayaka's triumph, and Sayaka who offered you advice in return, and you want her to *know* that. That you don't care just about her magic.

Even so, though...

This is *huge*.

You can still feel that ember of jealousy burning, ugly and dark, but it's dwarfed by your excitement. It's a pity you've got so much on your plate already, with the rest of your afternoon booked full and Sayaka trapped in school, because you *absolutely* want to dig into this. To find out how exactly Sayaka's magic can work, what she can do, and what you might be able to do *together*.

"Bah, don't worry about it," Sayaka says, rolling her eyes yet again, far too used to your shenanigans. A moment later, her expression fades into something more serious. "I mean. I know what it feels like, y'know?"

"Hm?" you say, glancing at her.

"Jealousy, I mean," Sayaka says. "I know what it's like to... just get stuck thinking about that sort of thing, 'round and 'round in circles. It's always been Madoka who pushed me out of that sort of mood, and I'm just passing on the favour. So yeah. I get it."

"I..." You trail off, blinking at Sayaka. Surprise, then realization percolates through you. She *would* know, wouldn't she? Being the least well-off of their group of friends, and then being forced to depend on Hitomi after Oriko burning down her house...

... yeah.

She *would* get it. It's what led her to becoming a magical girl, in many ways. Which you suppose is a lesson in and of itself, to be *careful* of the kind of decisions that sort of emotion can lead you down, but never to *ignore* things like that. Doubly so, given that you're a magical girl.

"Don't make a big deal of it," Sayaka says, flapping her hand at you and looking away. "Seriously, it's not."

"Alright," you say. You feel the grin returning to your face, just... simple gratitude and that sense of *kinship* and understanding.

"Shut up," Sayaka says, her cheeks tinging red.

"Alright, alright," you say, laughing and holding up both hands in surrender, and *also* resisting the urge to comment about how much like Kyouko she is. "Uhhh. So we really need to experiment with your magic more. But either it's tied to your *you*, or it doesn't work through your clone, so we need to schedule some time for figuring it out."

"During the trip to Kyushu later?" she offers, glancing vaguely southwards.

"I think that's the only free-ish time we have that's available soon, yeah," you say, frowning. "OK. There *is* one experiment we can try right now, since I'm doing Grief release experiments right now, anyway."

"Whether I can grab Grief you've already let go of?" Sayaka asks.

"Yeah," you say. "Might as well give it a shot, right? Since I'm already doing it."

"Worth the try," Sayaka agrees, frowning. "It's weird, though. Cleansing my own Soul Gem was... easy, like just reaching out and grabbing something off a shelf. Trying to cleanse *your* Soul Gem was like... my 'arm' couldn't even turn in that direction. Does that make sense?"

"Itty bitty little tyrannosaurs arms, got it," you say, nodding wisely and wiggling your hands at her, forearms tucked tight to your body.

"*No,*" Sayaka grumbles, long-suffering. "That's not- that's not even accurate. It's not a matter of *reach*, it's more like- my 'arm' doesn't even bend that way. Have you ever felt that sort of thing?"

You frown, thinking through your own experiences. Memories of the times you'd tried to make something out of Grief and simply *failed* come to mind, but it... doesn't feel the same as what Sayaka just described. You've never run into something like simply not have Grief answer your call. Even that first time you'd drained a Grief Seed -Hildegarde's Grief Seed- and found your magic failing to move the Witch itself, you hadn't felt like it was an inability for your magic to move in that metaphysical direction - rather, it had felt more like failing to lift yourself up by your bootstraps.

"I don't think so," you say slowly. "Also, I'm just saying, T-rex arms are noticeably not very flexible."

"*No,* and stop imagining me with T-rex arms, I know you are," Sayaka says. "Might be because I have a *copy* of your magic?"

"Maybe, but that doesn't feel right? Or not the complete explanation, anyway," you say, scrubbing your hand through your hair as you think. "I think... for a fair comparison, we might have to get you to talk to someone else whose power you've used a lot? See if you have the same limitations as they do, or *different* limitations. Mami, maybe? But you haven't really experimented that much with her magic, right?"

"Only a little bit," Sayaka says, scowling and gesturing down at herself. "Her magic is like... *ridiculously* high-skill. I think the power I've gotten the most use out of is Koizumi's."

"We could maybe arrange a meeting with her, then?" you say, nodding slowly. Koizumi Aki, with clones - case in point, the Sayaka clone you're speaking to right now. "If she's willing, anyway."

"Eh, maybe," Sayaka says. "I don't think *that's* a big deal, I can mess around with Mami's magic and talk to her. But *anyway*, that's enough about me, didn't you have science to get back to?"

"Didn't *we* have science to get back to, you mean," you say. "Grief release. I'll make it a small amount... and I'll go to the far end of Warehouse-kun to make sure that there isn't some issue with the Grief being in my range or something, yeah? I think if you stand at the edge of the courtyard and I'm at the diagonal it'll be just enough distance."

"Sounds like a plan," Sayaka says, her expression brightening.

You split up, Sayaka walking to the corner of the courtyard while you jog to the far end of the old warehouse, your footsteps scratching over the sand and echoing from rusting old rafters as you dart over weeds reaching for the meager sunlight filtering in. It takes you just a moment to reach the end of the building and summon a single marble of Grief from hammerspace.

"Sending it over!" you holler at Sayaka.

"Got it!" Sayaka calls back.

You can feel her jittering a little in impatience as the marble of Grief zips back towards her, and another quick confirmation with her later, you shove it past your control radius with that now-familiar, convulsive jerk.

And almost immediately, you feel *Grief under someone else's control*.

It's an immediate awareness, the same insistent, throbbing *pressure* in the back of your mind as a Witch's Barrier, the same cold, dripping, *slimy* nails-on-chalkboard sensation clawing up your spine that has your teeth grinding against each other as you bite back the urge to *take it back*.

Ah.

*That's* what it feels like when someone trespasses on your Wish, you suppose distantly.

The larger part of you, the *rational* part of you, rebels against the sensation. You'd *given* the Grief to Sayaka, *let* her copy your power. You don't mind her doing this sort of thing - jealousy aside, you *want* her to be able to do this sort of thing, even.

And almost as soon as those thoughts crystallize within your mind, you feel something indefinable *click*, and the discomfort instantly begins receding. Sensation returns to your limbs, warmth replacing the chill and smothering the shriek to a dull roar. You exhale hard, a knot in your chest you hadn't even noticed unspooling.

You push off the wall and pat your cheeks, finding your face clammy with sweat.

Today's a day for unpleasant realizations about yourself, you can't help but think to yourself as a thought and a scrap of Grief swipes the sweat from your face. But you're glad, at least, that this one came about out of line of sight of Sayaka. You wouldn't want her to see your reaction to her using your magic, after all: you don't want her to see your entirely unwarranted reaction to her *triumph*.

You take another deep breath, check yourself one more time, and stride back towards your friend. You can feel the buzz of *her* Grief, now a weight pressing upon the back of your mind, but nothing you can't ignore. And you manage a genuine smile for Sayaka as you step back out into the sunlight because dammit, you *are* happy for her.

"Seems like it worked?" you call.

"Yeah!" Sayaka says, answering your grin with one of her own. "Look!"

She holds her hand out, above which billows a cloud of Grief, loose and undifferentiated.

"Same as what it felt like cleansing my own Soul Gem," she notes. "How do you compact it down?"

"Just will it to, like... I dunno, squishing a sponge down, or something like that," you say. "I'm sorry, it's like asking how to lift your hand up or something, you know?"

"... yeah, alright, I'll try and figure it out," Sayaka says, and blinks. "Huh."

"Hm?" you say, glancing at the Grief still floating over her hand. "Figure out something?"

"... yeah... I mean, no, not about the compacting thing, but uh," Sayaka says. She points at herself. "I'm controlling Grief here, *and* with my real body in class. That's- that's not how my copied magic usually works."

"... *huh*," you say, staring at her. Right. Normally she has to juggle around individually copied powers and all, frequently with your help to carry the crystallized power jewels around. "Can you- wait, uh, hm. *Which* of your bodies actually has the magic?"

"This one," Sayaka says immediately. "*That* I'm sure of."

"But you definitely cleansed yourself in class," you say. "That's... weird. That's *really* weird. I mean- well, I suppose I always figured my magic was tied to my soul, given that the range on my magic is exactly the same as my, ah, Soul Gem radius, so... I guess it counts? But I would have thought it'd be the same with other magic as well."

"Yeah..." Sayaka says, looking distracted. "Yeah, it's... let me try and explore my own magic a bit? This is *weird*, to say the least, I want to double check with all the copied powers I've got. Uh. Let me know when you're doing your Grief release, I'll keep the antimagic ready."

"Sure," you say, nodding. You definitely understand the impulse to check when your *own* magic does something, after all. "Though I'll say that I'm going to start scaling up the release quantities. I maintain that it shouldn't be dangerous in any significant way, but it's having you as backup is just sensible."

"Yup," Sayaka says, distracted frown still on her face as she taps her foot against the floor, concrete rising to form another crude seat which she falls into.

You chuckle softly to yourself, then a little louder when she shoots a glare at you, the greasy sensation of antimagic gathering around her free hand as she points accusingly in your direction. You wipe the grin from your mouth and hold up both hands, conceding defeat - you *don't* want to get blasted with antimagic today, thank you very much, but you'll also take it as a sign that she's not intending to slack off on her backstop duties.

Back to it, then.

You've covered all the basic, safe level of Grief release, you think. You're vaguely concerned about pollution - even if it isn't *immediately* harmful, it might be that releasing Grief into the air is a possible contaminant, and a long term concern? You'll need to investigate that too, but *that* is difficult without records. Maybe there's some way to do the metaphysical equivalent of core samples, or tree ring climate estimates, or similar.

Things to think about later.

With the knowledge that the effects seem to be agnostic to location -well, discounting the accumulation of Grief in any given location- you feel confident that you can repeat the tests faster, and you scale up quickly. Two marbles seem to do nothing new, and neither does anything come of three, and so you work your way up past twenty marbles at a time before you notice any real difference: the seepage, the settling, whatever you want to call it, is lasting longer and longer.

You've taken to timing how long it takes for the Grief to fully dissipate on your phone, and that time has certainly been going up. The rate of the settling seems to scale proportionately to the both the *amount* of Grief as well as the density, and so does the rate of dissipation, but... the settling goes up *faster*. You could almost certainly work out an equation for this - it seems oddly straightforward, for something so intensely magical.

And even without writing out that equation and working out the coefficients, you can tell there's an inflection point coming up soon, where the rate of settling outpaces the rate of dissipation. It's somewhere past a hundred or so marbles of Grief, and... you have a sinking feeling that that inflection point is *exactly* the amount of Grief where you would be Grief spiralling. As the thought passes your mind, it just feels *correct*, a cold, inevitable certainty settling into your bones, calculations be damned.

It's a good thing you can cleanse yourself, you suppose, but it's never quite nice to be reminded in that way, and so you do just that. No reason not to be sure, you suppose, and with your Soul Gem shining and pristine, you're a little tempted to jump straight ahead to that threshold. But that wouldn't be proper procedure.

You keep working at it steadily, Sayaka keeping an eye on you while trying to figure out her newly copied magic.

The Grief thickens in the air, as you pass a hundred marbles. There's no discrete change, no grand shift, but it's noticeable, balancing on the very cusp of stability. And then, on the next attempt, the balance tips. The Grief... *curdles* in the air, metastasizing into what you know you have to call *miasma*.

"Huh," Sayaka says, craning her head back to stare.

"Yeah," you say, likewise staring upwards. "Huh."

It doesn't *look* much like anything to your eyes: a faint, hazy film that's just barely there, seeming more like a trick of the light than anything - a distortion in the air you can just barely see when you move your head, but not quite *visible* when you look at it head on. If you had to guess, an ordinary human wouldn't be able to see it at all.

But your magical senses are a whole other matter. It's *emptiness*, the kind of yawning void of a sinkhole or the empty hunger of deepest winter, the endless spiral down the drain and into a creeping cold and dark that consumes not out of malice but because it simply *is*. It's the hollow, frozen despair at the end of all things, a black hole clawing at reality with neither malice nor thought, merely inevitability.

A tiny patch of despair, sunken into reality.

And yet...

It feels *fragile*. Weak, perhaps, but that might be assigning it more intentionality than it actually deserves: but for all of its desolate hunger, it feels gossamer thin, as if the slightest brush might disrupt it. Because it's *just* barely on the cusp of stability you assume, because it formed out of the barest minimum amount of Grief.

"Is that... what you expected?" Sayaka ventures, eyes still locked on it.

"A miasma," you say, and grimace, looking at Sayaka. "Sort of, yeah. You know how I had, uh, all sorts of weird knowledge, even before I contracted?"

"Yeah?" Sayaka says, apparently noticing your attention and turning to raise an eyebrow at you. "You knew about this, too?"

"I did, yeah, but I wasn't totally sure it *would* actually do it," you say. "I also know what... *should* happen if it gains more Grief, yeah. Though now that I say that, I feel like it's almost guaranteed that I affected the result by having an expectation on the result at all."

Sayaka snorts.

"And I can't replicate the results independently because now I've seen it," she says. "For shame, Sabrina, contaminating the experiment."

"You were part of it from the very beginning," you point out.

"Details, details," Sayaka says airily. "So, what *is* it?"

You hesitate for a moment. There's a possibility Kyuubey's observing you - you haven't put up a privacy ward, after all, given that by the very nature of your experiment, you need to push things beyond the boundaries of the range you can affect. But then, on the balance of things, *if* it's observing, you don't think it's necessarily a *bad* thing if it knows that an... *alternative* to the current system exists.

If you were in the *inverse* situation, if this were the world of wraiths and miasma, you would die before revealing *anything* of the existence of Witches. But you're not. You *know* the direction of the Incubators' preferences, after all.

"A miasma. The result of sufficient Grief concentrations in the world, formed by, uh, semi-natural magical processes. I *think*. I'm pretty sure, but not totally sure," you say. You frown, considering the miasma for a moment, then continue in a steady, even tone. "This... this would be the first sign of what is to come, if Witches and Familiars didn't exist to horf down all the Grief."

Sayaka's expression flickers, going from drawn concern to an eyetwitch of annoyance.

"I want to reiterate my earlier point," Sayaka says. "You are *such* a *dork*."

"Oh, yes. It comes naturally to me, you know?" you say, nodding solemnly before letting your expression soften, raw honesty rearing its head. "A dork of the highest order. And I think... if it *didn't*, I'd probably want to practice it until it became second nature."

"I'm going to regret asking this, but... what, because you're the big bad scary Grief controller?" Sayaka says.

"Yep!" you say cheerfully. "Luckily, I *am* a colossal dork, and proud of i- *ow*! What was that for?"

You scowl at Sayaka, rubbing your forehead - which *happens* to be exactly where Sayaka flicked a pebble at.

"For picking at your scabs again," Sayaka says, rolling her eyes. "Stop that."

"That- that wasn't even a *scab*, or anything you could remotely describe as an issue, just me remarking on myself and what I want for myself," you complain. "I protest this treatment! I have done *nothing* of the sort to deserve it!"

"Keep telling yourself that," Sayaka says. "Anyway. So... if that's a 'sign of what is to come', then what *is* to come?"

"Nothing *too* dangerous," you say. "About on level with a strong Familiar, I'd say, and definitely nothing either of us alone can't handle."

"And you're not telling me because you don't want to bias the results further?" Sayaka asks. "But aren't *you* biasing it already?"

"Oh yes," you say, scrubbing a hand through your hair and scowling at a small cloud in the sky as you think through the phrasing. "It's... mm. So this is largely my own philosophizing about it, as opposed to anything I know for sure, OK? Magic is affected by belief - I don't know whether it's accurate to say that magic *is* belief, but it's something deeply tied up with belief and... I dunno, intelligent life. We *will* the world to change, we *believe* it so intensely that our souls shape the world. Do you follow?"

"More or less, yeah?" Sayaka says.

"So... my point is, I suspect that by existing at all, I've *already* biased this whole thing," you say. "Maybe if I went off to the other side of the solar system, or something? Maybe *if* there were some other tradition or belief or *expectation* existing, then things might balance out, but I... don't know if there *is* such a thing."

"Right..." Sayaka says. "So if it's already biased, you *can* tell me?"

You waggle your hand.

"This is the fuzzy edge of magic, right? Like the bleeding edge of technology, but it's magic, so the more advanced the magic, the more ambiguous it is," you say. "For all that I think that I've already biased it by existing, *maybe* not telling you might change it." You raise a finger and pause to let that sink in, and as Sayaka nods, you continue. "Also, by *not* telling you, I can maintain an air of mystery and suspense."

Sayaka pinches the bridge of her nose and exhales, a long, gusty sigh.

"I can have more than one motivation at work!" you say brightly.

"I think I must have been an axe murderer in a past life," Sayaka says, staring at the warehouse wall. "I don't know what else I could have done to deserve this."

"... too much?" you say. "I'll ease up a little."

"Hm? Oh, no, no, I'm just playing along," Sayaka says, grinning. "I'm the straight man here, no?"

"Ah," you say, nodding. "... are you *sure* you're straight?"

Sayaka rolls her eyes, slicing a hand through the air.

"Alright, alright, *now* that's enough of that," she says. "What's next?"

"What's next is... can you fly up there and swipe it or something? That *should* disrupt it, and I want to confirm," you say. You wave awkwardly over your shoulder. "I'd do it myself, but uh. My everything deals with Grief, y'know?"

"And you want to know how it goes with a regular magic girl," Sayaka says, nodding in understanding. "Lemme put aside my copy of your magic first."

You nod, and wait patiently - it takes a couple moments, and then Sayaka's soaring up into the sky on a trail of cerulean blue, spiralling upwards to the barely-there miasma. You focus, watching intently as she approaches, the miasma unreacting, right up until Sayaka swings her arm through it. The miasma seems to *cling* to her for a heartbeat, stretching... and *popping*, some indefinable structure to it irrevocably lost.

The Grief splatters with the motion of Sayaka's arm, as if she'd swung through a giant balloon full of water, and the miasma dissolves without fuss, its constituent Grief dissipating into the surroundings without any further fuss.

About what you'd hoped for, then, and you nod at Sayaka as she swoops back towards the ground.

"I *felt* that even without your magic," she reports. "I guess that makes sense. Grief and all."

"Yeah," you agreed. "Still, that was important to confirm. Thanks, Sayaka."

"Yeah, yeah," Sayaka says, waving it off. "You're welcome. 's why I'm here."

"Alright," you say. "Next test. Forming a new miasma, and feeding it more Grief."

"Gotcha," Sayaka says. "I'll keep an eye out."

Back to it, then. A thought summons more Grief from hammerspace, and you launch it once more into the air, thoughts turning inward.

Now that you know the threshold above which miasma starts to form, you can't help but notice that the amounts of Grief the Soujus' technique works with just... won't ever rise to any sort of dangerous level. You're not going to rigorously prove that it's the same amount of Grief where you yourself would be Grief spiralling, but it's certainly at a level where any magical girl would be in *bad* shape. The Soujus would never deal with that sort of quantities.

There's still the open question of what, exactly, Grief does when you disperse it into the surroundings, but in terms of immediate danger, there would appear to be none. At least, not from the process of cleansing itself.

Hrmph.

That's a thought for later.

Now, you allow another miasma to form, just beyond the reach of your own Grief manipulation, and you experiment with feeding it Grief.

It *draws in* Grief, you notice: again, not unexpected, but important to confirm. Your boots scrape across the sandy courtyard as you work to check the range, but if there's a limit, you don't find it, Grief released even from the opposite side of Warehouse-kun drifting slowly but inexorably towards the miasma. The pull doesn't even seem to fall off with distance, merely the *amount* of Grief you release, the bottomless, empty hunger of the miasma drinking deep and even drawing in the Grief you'd previously allowed to dissipate.

And with the miasma constantly getting fed, you can feel it teetering once more upon the precipice of some other threshold, the empty hunger forming *structure* in the mindless way of rocks piling upon each other, of crystal growing out of cooling rock before dissolving again. Order formed of nothing.

Just a bit more Grief, to push it over the edge. Another two marbles, cast skyward, and released to be drawn into the miasma, and...

That patch of air shivers.

And it's there.

The white of deepest winter, the white of bleached bone, the white of death. Too-long limbs and cloth that drapes wrongly, empty robes upon jittering emptiness, crowned with shimmering flecks of nothing upon a featureless head. It sits as if cross-legged, hollow robes over alabaster nihility. It doesn't float in the air, it simply... stutters. It falls, and then it doesn't, seated upon skipped frames in the skein of reality. Serene, untouched by anything so paltry as gravity.

*Wraith*.

You sense its hunger. You sense the yawning abyss within it, the void that will never be filled. You sense its urge to kill, to destroy, to drink in light and life and hope.

You sense nothing at all.

It does not want, for it has no mind.

It does not hunger, for it has no stomach.

It simply exists, and its nature is to devour. In the same way that the cold saps away heat, in the same way that a vacuum draws in air, in the same way that a black hole tears stars asunder. In the same way that deepest despair will drown out all that is good in the world.

All that, for something you now know you could crush with a thought.

You sense... expectation. Attention.

You control Grief. *All* Grief.

And here is despair given form.

It will obey you, as long as you only tell it to destroy. To consume.

It will obey... *you*?

No. Not you.

Not *you*.

*Dedolere*.

Where she walks, Grief follows. Where she rests, despair falls. Where she slumbers, hope flourishes.

You stand astride your own grave, your Witch slumbering at the root of your soul. Your magic and your Witch are intertwined, and here and now, in this moment, you are very,

*very*​

aware of that fact.​

A blade passes through the wraith, and dissolves in motes of cerulean light.

You blink, and you're aware of Sayaka standing in front of you. All you can see is her back, her cape fluttering in the breeze and sword handle held in an outstretched, ready stance, and even as you watch, she dismisses it, forming another, her eyes unerringly on the wraith - or rather, where the wraith *was*.

You take in a breath that's perhaps a little more shaky than it normally is.

"Sabrina?" Sayaka asks, her attention unwavering.

"I'm alright," you say. "I'm alright. It- the wraith's dead. Thank you, Sayaka."

You can feel your breath in your lungs once more, your heartbeat in your throat, the blessed warmth of *life*. And beneath it all, your own Witch, slumbering even as your magic reaches *through* her. Of course your magic has a Witchy feel. Of course you can control Grief as if it's an extension of your own body.

Because it is. Because the domain of Grief is *yours*. It *will* be, and it always has been. Grieve no more, for salvation is come.

Once and future, here and now. Once upon a never, an ending that will never be.

Well. Alright, then.

"You sure, Sabrina?" Sayaka asks, lowering her sword warily.

"Yeah," you say. "Yeah, I am."
